# Ten Real-Life Decisions About Neighbors

Decisions between people who did not choose each other, cannot easily leave, and
will still be adjacent in five years. Same format as the companion files: the
situation, the live options, and the tension that keeps the choice from
resolving.

---

## 1. The tree on the boundary

Their sycamore blocks your afternoon light, drops on your roof, and is
structurally sound. Cutting the overhanging branches is your legal right and
would ruin the shape of a forty-year-old tree.

- **Options:** cut back to the line; ask them to prune it; share the cost of
  professional shaping; live with it.
- **What makes it hard:** you are entitled to do the thing that will be
  remembered every time they look out of the window, and enforcing a boundary
  is how most long neighbor disputes begin.

## 2. Noise, and whether to escalate

Music until 1am on weeknights. You have mentioned it twice, politely, and it
improves for a fortnight each time.

- **Options:** speak to them again; write to them; complain to the council or
  landlord; soundproof at your own expense.
- **What makes it hard:** the informal route preserves the relationship and has
  visibly stopped working, and the formal route solves the noise by ending any
  chance of an informal route ever again.

## 3. The dog

It barks all day when they are out, or it is loose and has run at your child.
The owners are attached to it and defensive.

- **Options:** raise it with them; report it; document incidents in case it
  escalates; adjust your own routine around it.
- **What makes it hard:** reporting an animal can end with it destroyed, and not
  reporting one means the record is empty on the day it bites someone.

## 4. Reporting something official

The loft conversion has no permit, or the number of people living in the flat
breaches the lease, or you suspect a child or an elderly person there is not
being cared for.

- **Options:** report it; ask them about it first; report only the welfare
  concern; do nothing.
- **What makes it hard:** the rules exist because self-interested neighbors are
  unreliable judges, and you are the only person positioned to see this — which
  makes you both the right witness and an interested party.

## 5. The neighbor who may not be coping

The bins have not moved in three weeks. The curtains stay shut. She is
seventy-eight, proud, and has told you before that she manages fine.

- **Options:** knock and ask directly; contact her family; call social services
  or a welfare line; keep an eye and do nothing yet.
- **What makes it hard:** intruding on a competent adult's privacy is a real
  wrong, and waiting for certainty means the intervention arrives after the
  fall rather than before it.

## 6. The short-term let next door

The flat has become a rotating stream of strangers with suitcases at midnight,
or a home business with vans blocking the street. It is their property and
their income.

- **Options:** talk to the owner; report it to the platform or council; organize
  neighbors collectively; accept it.
- **What makes it hard:** they are making legitimate use of what they own, and
  what you are protecting — a street where you know who lives there — is real
  but has no standing anyone has to recognize.

## 7. The shared repair they will not pay for

The party wall, the fence, the shared drain. Your half of the cost is
manageable; they say they cannot afford it, or simply will not engage.

- **Options:** pay the whole thing yourself; pursue them formally; do the
  minimum patch; leave it and let it deteriorate.
- **What makes it hard:** paying for both preserves the peace and establishes,
  for every future repair, that you are the one who pays.

## 8. The neighborhood group chat

What started as parcel deliveries is now photographs of strangers walking past,
speculation about who does not belong, and talk of calling the police.

- **Options:** push back in the chat; leave quietly; stay and moderate what you
  can; start something separate.
- **What makes it hard:** the group genuinely does help people, and objecting
  makes you the neighbor who is difficult about safety — which is why the drift
  usually goes unchallenged.

## 9. Opposing the development

Forty new flats behind your garden. It would take your light and your view; it
would also house forty households in a place with nowhere to live.

- **Options:** object formally; object only to the height and massing; support
  it; stay out of it.
- **What makes it hard:** your objection would be sincere and your interest is
  obvious, and the mechanism that lets you object is the same one that has made
  housing scarce for the people who would live there.

## 10. Where your camera points

The doorbell camera covers your porch, most of the pavement, and their front
door. It has already been useful once.

- **Options:** keep it as is; angle it to your property only; tell them it is
  there; take it down.
- **What makes it hard:** security only works by recording the approach, and the
  approach is where other people's ordinary comings and goings happen.

---

## Why these ten

They separate what makes neighbor problems distinctively hard:

- **You cannot exit** — every option in 1, 2, and 7 is priced by the fact that
  you will both still be here afterward.
- **Rights against relations** — 1, 7, and 10 all involve doing something you
  are fully entitled to do, where entitlement is not the question.
- **Interested witnesses** — 3, 4, and 5 make you the only person who can see
  the problem and someone with a stake in the outcome, at once.
- **Private interest wearing public clothes** — 6, 8, and 9 turn on a claim
  about the common good that also happens to be exactly what suits you.
