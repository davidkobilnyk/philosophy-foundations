# Ten Real-Life Decisions About Friendship

Decisions inside voluntary bonds with no contract, no institution, and no
formal way to leave — where nothing anywhere specifies what is owed. Same
format as the companion files: the situation, the live options, and the tension
that keeps the choice from resolving.

---

## 1. Lending the money

They have asked for a real amount. You have it. They have been vague about
when it comes back and you both know the friendship is the collateral.

- **Options:** lend it; give it and say it is a gift; lend a smaller amount you
  are willing to lose; decline and offer another kind of help.
- **What makes it hard:** a loan makes them a debtor and a gift makes them a
  recipient, and neither is a thing a friendship easily contains — but refusing
  says something too.

## 2. Telling them what you know about their partner

You saw something, or someone told you. They are happy. You might be wrong
about what it meant.

- **Options:** tell them plainly; tell them what you saw without interpreting;
  confront the partner first; say nothing.
- **What makes it hard:** you would want to be told, which is the strongest
  argument and not evidence about them — and the messenger is frequently the
  only casualty.

## 3. The friendship you are always the one to start

Every meeting for two years has begun with your message. They are warm when
you meet and never initiate.

- **Options:** keep initiating; stop and see what happens; name it directly;
  reduce it to the level they sustain.
- **What makes it hard:** effort is not evenly distributed in any friendship
  worth having, and there is no threshold anywhere that tells you when
  asymmetry has become an answer.

## 4. The mistake you can see and they cannot

The marriage, the business, the move. You think it will go badly and they have
already decided and are telling you happily.

- **Options:** say it once, clearly; say it if asked; ask questions instead of
  objecting; support them and keep it to yourself.
- **What makes it hard:** they will remember what you said either way, and the
  friend who says nothing and the friend who says it once are indistinguishable
  right up until it goes wrong.

## 5. Letting it drift, or naming it

You have not spoken properly in a year. Nothing happened. Getting back in touch
means acknowledging the gap; not doing it means the friendship quietly ends.

- **Options:** reach out as though nothing happened; reach out and name the
  gap; accept it has become something smaller; let it go.
- **What makes it hard:** friendships end almost entirely by attrition rather
  than decision, so the only way to lose one deliberately is to keep not acting.

## 6. When two friends fall out

They have stopped speaking. Each has told you their version, each is partly
right, and both would like you to keep seeing them.

- **Options:** stay close to both and discuss neither; take the side you think
  is right; withdraw from both until it settles; tell each what you think.
- **What makes it hard:** neutrality is itself a verdict to the person who
  believes they were wronged, and there is no arrangement here that costs you
  nothing.

## 7. The friend who needs more than you have

The crisis is genuine and it has lasted eighteen months. The calls are long,
frequent, and one-directional, and you have started avoiding the phone.

- **Options:** keep going; say honestly what you can manage; help them find
  support that is not you; step back.
- **What makes it hard:** need does not distribute itself according to what
  anyone can supply, and the point at which you stop is the point at which they
  will remember you stopped.

## 8. The secret you are keeping from your partner

Your friend told you something in confidence. You do not keep things from your
partner, and this is not yours.

- **Options:** keep it entirely; tell your partner and ask them to hold it; ask
  your friend for permission; tell your friend you cannot hold it.
- **What makes it hard:** both relationships make an implicit claim to complete
  candor, and you agreed to the second one without checking it against the
  first.

## 9. Vouching for them

They have asked you to refer them for a job, or to introduce them to someone
who trusts you. You are fond of them and you have doubts about the work.

- **Options:** refer them warmly; refer them with a caveat; decline kindly;
  introduce without endorsing.
- **What makes it hard:** a recommendation spends credibility that is not
  really yours to spend, and declining tells your friend something about your
  opinion of them that you would never say aloud.

## 10. The friend who has done something genuinely wrong

Not to you. To someone else, and it is serious, and they have not really
reckoned with it.

- **Options:** end the friendship; stay and say what you think; stay and say
  nothing; stay involved only on condition that they address it.
- **What makes it hard:** loyalty that survives nothing is not loyalty, and
  loyalty that survives everything is how people are enabled — and the person
  they wronged has no say in which one you are.

---

## Why these ten

They isolate what makes friendship different from family, work, and dating:

- **Nothing specifies what is owed** — 3, 5, and 7 have no norm, no contract,
  and no institution to appeal to; whatever you decide becomes the standard.
- **Truth nobody asked for** — 2, 4, and 9 all involve information the other
  person has not requested and may be worse off holding.
- **Asymmetry with no accounting** — 1, 3, and 7 run an imbalance that both of
  you can see and neither of you is allowed to name.
- **Loyalty with more than one claimant** — 6, 8, and 10 make you answerable to
  someone outside the friendship at the same time.
